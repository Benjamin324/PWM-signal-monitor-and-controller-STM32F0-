ECE 355 Lab

Lab Grading Scheme

Student Teams

All of lab-related work in each section must be done in a team setting, where each team consists of two students in that section. One project report must be submitted per team.

Mark Division

Your overall lab mark is divided as follows:
✓ Introductory Lab Part 2 demo: (10%) __________
✓ Project demo: (30%) __________ * see Note [1]
✓ Project report: (60%) __________
✓ Total (100%) __________

* Note [1]: ADC = 5%, DAC = 5%, LED Display = 10%, USER button = 5%, everything works together = 5%.

IMPORTANT: You are strongly encouraged to demo any project portion in its own as soon as you get it to work during any of your lab sessions (as opposed to waiting for the entire project to be completed before its demo). For example, to demo the ADC portion on its own, you can simply print the ADC digital output value on the Eclipse console to show that it's consistent with the ADC analog input value measured by a scope.

Demonstration

  • You are expected to demonstrate your Introductory Lab Part 2 by the end of your second lab session.
  • You are expected to demonstrate your Project by the end of your fourth (last) lab session.
  • You must demonstrate your work to the TA for the section you are registered in!
  • Late demonstrations will NOT be accepted.

Project Report

The lab project report is due by 11:59 PM on the section-specific dates (see Time Table for details). Please submit it via your lab section's Brightspace website (Note: Every lab section has its own Brightspace website). Late submissions will NOT be accepted.

The lab project report marks are distributed as follows:
✓ Problem Description/Specifications: (5) __________
✓ Design/Solution (15) __________
✓ Testing/Results (10) __________
✓ Discussion (15) __________
✓ Code Design and Documentation: (15) __________
✓ Total (60) __________

*Please include this marking form on the cover of your lab report.

In general, lab project report evaluations will depend on technical contents (problem description, solutions, discussion, etc) as well as format (professional appearance, writing style, clarity, etc). Your report must be complete enough for the marker to be able to understand and reproduce your design without consulting other sources. Your typed report should include at least the following items (information provided for each item must be precise and concise):
  • Problem Description/Specifications. State objectives and specifications of the lab being solved in your own words.
  • Design/Solution (with diagrams). Explain your design methodology, such as your design steps, lab work partitioning, special techniques used, etc. Clearly explain any special programming considerations (e.g., explain how and why you set up control registers, how and why you check for flags, etc). You need to include all block diagrams and schematics needed to understand and reproduce your design. They must be drawn using appropriate software tools (not by hand).
  • Testing/Results. Clearly explain and justify your procedure for testing the project to ensure its correctness. Discuss your results and observations, e.g., measurement range, measurement resolution, measurement errors, system limitations, design limitations, etc.
  • Discussion. Briefly comment on your design specifications, assumptions, shortcomings, and included extra features (if any). Highlight any differences between your design and stated specifications. Summarize lessons learned while doing the lab and provide advice.
  • Appendices. As an appendix, include full listing of your well-commented source code. If needed, attach any other additional information that can help others understand and reproduce your design.
  • References. All printed and online sources used in the process of completing the lab project must be properly referenced. Using any portion of any lab-related work done by any other group is NOT allowed.
